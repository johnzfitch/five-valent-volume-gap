\begin{center}
\estitle{The Volume Gap at Five-Valent Vertices}\\[4pt]
\essubtitle{Every five-valent Ashtekar--Lewandowski vertex whose edge tangents are in
general position is zero or one of three operators. The gap closes when the two triple products through the
distinguished edge carry unequal weights, and survives when they do not compete; at
equal weights it survives on every class we can reach.}\\[10pt]
{\normalsize John Zachary Fitch}\\[1pt]
{\small\texttt{zack@internetuniverse.org}}
\end{center}

\esrule

\begin{quote}\small
\textsc{Abstract.} Whether the volume operator of loop quantum gravity has a
spectral gap had been addressed at valence four semiclassically, numerically and at
leading order in the spins, and at valence five and higher only numerically:
Brunnemann and Rideout observed that some embeddings produce eigenvalues accumulating
at zero and others do not, and that the decaying ones are exactly those whose reduced
sign factors contain $\pm4$. We explain this rule for the Ashtekar--Lewandowski
operator and prove most of it in the convention in which spins are assigned to the
edges of a fixed embedding. Up to relabelling, every five-valent vertex operator whose
edge tangents are in general position (no three coplanar) is zero or $-4i[\alpha\,J_1\cdot(J_2\times J_3)+\beta\,J_1\cdot(J_4\times
J_5)]$ with $(\alpha,\beta)\in\{(2,0),(2,2),(4,2)\}$, and ``contains $\pm4$'' means
$t=\beta/\alpha=\frac12$. One direction of the rule is proved in full: for every
$|t|\notin\{0,1\}$ the gap closes, through a dimerised chain with two edge modes,
exponentially in the spin; at the Ashtekar--Lewandowski value $t=\frac12$ the rate is
$\qm\asymp N^{3/2}2^{-N}$. The other direction is proved for $t=0$, where the gap is
exactly $2\sqrt3$, and in part for $t=1$: there the gap is proved when the
distinguished spin is $\frac12$, $1$, $\frac32$ or $2$, on every tuple in which three
of the other four spins are at most $3$ (where it grows linearly in the distinguished
spin with the sharp slope $4\sqrt{10-6\sqrt2}$), and on several further infinite classes,
and it is certified for all spins up to $19$. The rest of $t=1$ is stated as a
conjecture: a uniform gap, growing with that slope in the distinguished spin and like
$M/\ln M$ in the largest spin, for which we prove the upper bound. In Brunnemann and
Rideout's own convention, with the spins sorted, the decay is proved for $6$ of the
$60$ sign vectors (up to sign) that contain $\pm4$. Along the way we prove the sharp
four-valent law $\lambda^2\ge j_{\max}(j_{\max}+1)/4$ for the nonzero eigenvalues $\lambda$ of
$J_1\cdot(J_2\times J_3)$, whose constant and minimisers Brunnemann and Thiemann found
numerically, through a factorisation into a hydrogen Stark matrix; a factorial gap
closing at a six-valent vertex in general position; and a uniform gap for the
Rovelli--Smolin operator on every five-valent vertex with an edge of spin $\frac12$,
which includes the vertices on which we prove that the Ashtekar--Lewandowski gap closes. What is new is the proofs, the mechanisms and the sharp constants. The
computer-assisted steps are exact rational, interval, or rigorously bounded
floating-point certificates, and all ship with replayable scripts.

\smallskip\noindent\textsc{MSC 2020.} 83C45 (primary); 81Q10, 47B36.
\end{quote}
