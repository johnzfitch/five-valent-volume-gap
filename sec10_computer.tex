\section{The computer-assisted steps}\label{sec:computer}

Several proofs end in a finite computation. This section says what each computation
decides, in which arithmetic, and where its script is. The scripts and outputs are at
\url{https://github.com/johnzfitch/five-valent-volume-gap} (release \texttt{v1.0}, which
also carries the outputs too large for the repository): \texttt{code/} holds the code
written for this paper, \texttt{certificates/} the certificates on which the proofs rest,
each subfolder with the checksums of its source, and \texttt{SHA256SUMS.txt} the
checksums of everything.

\subsection{Three kinds of certificate}

An \emph{exact} certificate decides a statement in rational arithmetic: the signs of the
pivots of an $LDL^T$ factorisation (Sylvester's law of inertia), the rank of a rational
matrix, or the signs of the coefficients of a polynomial after substituting nonnegative
shift variables that encode the constraints. An \emph{interval} certificate evaluates
an inequality with outward rounding (mpmath's interval type or Arb balls, or binary64
with every operation followed by one step of \texttt{nextafter} in the safe direction)
and checks it strictly. A \emph{rigorous-float} certificate computes in binary64 and
bounds the error a priori: the matrix is perturbed by the rounding of square roots
(Weyl's inequality), a congruence $X^T(\widehat A-tI)X$ is formed and its entrywise error
bounded with Higham's $\gamma_k$~\cite[Lemma~3.5]{Higham}, and strict Gershgorin
separation of the computed matrix then fixes the inertia of $A-tI$ by Sylvester's law;
the kernel dimension it is compared with is computed exactly.

The error model of the rigorous-float certificates needs each floating-point entry
$y$ of the matrix to approximate the exact square root of its rational squared link $r$
with relative error at most $2.0001u$, where $u=2^{-53}$ is the unit roundoff. The certificates of \cref{thm:tone}(h) verify
this rather than assume it: each entry is computed as \texttt{math.sqrt} of the
rational, and is then checked in exact rational arithmetic,
$r(1-E)^2\le y^2\le r(1+E)^2$ with $E=2.0001\cdot2^{-53}$, before it is used; the run
stops if any check fails. All $1\,843\,571$ tuples with spins at most $19$ and the
$430\,481$ tuples with $j_1=\frac52$ and $39\le2j_{\max}\le70$ pass, with the same
kernel dimensions and level minima as an earlier run that computed the square roots
differently.

\subsection{Our own code}

The constructions of \cref{sec:operator} were written for this paper and import
nothing from the certificate folders: \texttt{code/su2.py} builds the operators from
the Hermitian generators on the tensor product, with the invariant space realised as
the highest-weight vectors of weight $j_N$ in the first $N-1$ legs, and evaluates
Racah's formula for $6j$ symbols in exact rationals; \texttt{code/grid.py} builds the
grid of \cref{prop:grid}. \texttt{code/validate.py} checks the four-valent closed form
against brute force and against Racah recoupling, the identity
$\hat q_{IJK}=-4iJ_I\cdot(J_J\times J_K)$, the reduction \eqref{eq:sigma}, and the grid
against the full ten-triple operator built from integer tangents of each orbit
($1\,332$ cases, relative agreement $3\times10^{-15}$). \texttt{code/checks.py} checks
the identities and constants quoted in the text, \texttt{code/reproduce.py} the
published numbers of \cref{sec:operator}, and \texttt{code/nongeneric.py} the counts of
\cref{rem:nongeneric}.

\subsection{Related work and priority}\label{subsec:related}

Besides the papers discussed in \cref{sec:intro}, the volume spectrum has been studied
through closed formulas and recoupling theory~\cite{Thiemann1998,DPR1996,Meissner2006,
MajorSeifert2002,CCM2002,AMM2013,MMA2014,Sherif2026,BRprelim}, through quantum and
semiclassical tetrahedra and polyhedra~\cite{Barbieri1998,BaezBarrett1999,BDS2011,
Haggard2013,BM2017a,BM2017b}, and for the Rovelli--Smolin form~\cite{RS1995,Kis2021}. The
operator $J_1\cdot(J_2\times J_3)$ is older than loop quantum gravity: Chakrabarti
proposed its eigenvalues, in place of a pair Casimir, as labels for the states of three
coupled angular momenta~\cite[p.~301]{Chakrabarti1964}, and L\'evy-Leblond and
L\'evy-Nahas~\cite{LLLN1965} studied the symmetric coupling scheme it generates.

We read in full the papers on the volume operator and on the mixed product cited in this
paragraph and in \cref{sec:intro} (for Rovelli and Smolin, the arXiv version), with these
exceptions: of L\'evy-Leblond and L\'evy-Nahas~\cite{LLLN1965}, Aquilanti, Caligiana and
Cavalli~\cite{ACC2003} and Bendjoudi and Mebarki~\cite{BM2017a,BM2017b} we have read only
the abstracts, and the two-page erratum to Rovelli and Smolin~\cite{RS1995} we could not
obtain. We also screened by title and abstract the records on INSPIRE that cite
Brunnemann and Rideout, Brunnemann and Thiemann, and Bianchi and Haggard. In none of this
did we find a proof of a volume gap or of its absence beyond numerics, a statement of the
normal form, or the identification of the four-valent matrix with a hydrogen Stark matrix.
The priority statements of \cref{sec:intro} are limited accordingly: the two papers we
know only in abstract, \cite{LLLN1965} and \cite{ACC2003}, are where an earlier
appearance of the Stark structure would be most likely, and our statements do not cover
them.
